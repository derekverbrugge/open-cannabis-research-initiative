Research agenda

Our mission

Cannabis research remains fragmented, with important gaps in evidence for clinical and patient decision-making. The Open Cannabis Research Initiative seeks to expand independent, accessible and scientifically rigorous investigation of cannabinoids, terpenes, formulation chemistry, therapeutic applications, adverse effects and long-term outcomes.

Our objective is not to promote consumption or establish predetermined conclusions, but to empower patients and clinicians with evidence for informed, individualized decisions. PTSD is our initial research priority, not the limit of our scientific interests.

Proposed clinical case report · Open-science framework

Research questions

  • Does individual terpene composition alter acute anxiety or functional impairment at matched THC exposure?
  • Do formulations differ in trauma-related nightmares, flashbacks or sleep architecture?
  • Is sustained nightmare relief associated with improved trauma processing and durable clinical outcomes?

Study design

Begin with analytical characterization and toxicology review. Qualified investigators would design randomized, blinded comparisons of purified THC, placebo, terpene-only controls, individual terpene–THC combinations and characterized mixtures, with matched THC exposure.

Outcomes

  • Anxiety, paranoia, flashback frequency and severity
  • Nightmares, dream recall, sleep quality and sleep architecture
  • Attention, memory, psychomotor performance and daily functioning
  • Longitudinal PTSD severity, trauma processing and persistence of improvement
  • Adverse events, tolerance, dependence and discontinuation effects

Proposed work packages

  1. Systematic evidence mapping and analytical characterization.
  2. Preclinical safety and pharmacology assessment where justified.
  3. Investigator-designed controlled human interaction studies.
  4. PTSD-specific feasibility and longitudinal efficacy studies, subject to prior safety findings.

Candidate measures include CAPS-5, PCL-5, sleep diaries, polysomnography and validated cognitive tasks. Investigators would determine eligibility, sample size, endpoints and statistical analysis before enrollment.

Safeguards

Ethics approval, informed consent, medical oversight, preregistration, independent analysis and publication rights are essential. No self-experimentation or recruitment is conducted here.

Multi-institutional research and independent replication

Encourage multiple institutions to investigate the same terpene–THC interactions and PTSD outcomes through independently conducted studies. Complementary studies may investigate alternative formulations, populations, delivery methods and longitudinal outcomes.

  • Independent replication using comparable protocols, reference formulations and endpoints.
  • Multisite trials where appropriate, with shared eligibility criteria and standardized assessments.
  • Independent methodological approaches to test the robustness of findings.
  • Preregistered hypotheses, statistical analysis plans and transparent reporting of positive, negative and inconclusive results.
  • Cross-institutional evidence synthesis, including assessment of heterogeneity and reproducibility.

Shared protocols, analytical specifications and aggregate results should be openly available where feasible. Individual participant data must remain subject to consent, ethics approval, privacy requirements and appropriate data governance. Each institution retains scientific independence and responsibility for regulatory compliance.

Research accessibility and clinical education

Encourage lawful access to standardized research materials for qualified investigators, nonexclusive institutional funding, publication of adverse and inconclusive findings, accessible protocols and literature, clinically useful formulation comparisons and patient participation in identifying research questions. Clearly distinguish established evidence from preliminary findings and speculation. Evidence summaries should support shared decision-making, not replace clinical care.

Research advocacy only. Cannabis is not an established PTSD treatment; discuss care with a qualified clinician.
